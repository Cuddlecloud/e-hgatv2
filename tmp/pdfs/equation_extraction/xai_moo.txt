                 Abstract                                                         
                                                                                  
                 The Energy-Efficient Job Shop Scheduling Problem (EEJSP) is a scientifically
                 significant and practically impactful problem that has attracted substantial
                 attention over the past decade. In this problem, jobs are composed of a
                 sequence of operations that must be processed on either all or a designated
                 subset of machines. The complexity lies in jointly determining the operation
                 sequence on each machine and the processing speed assigned to each
                 operation. These speed settings influence both the production time
                 (makespan) and energy consumption [1], leading to a multi-objective
                 optimization (MOO) formulation aimed at minimizing both.         
                                                                                  
                 This combinatorial problem becomes even more intensive due to the high-
                 dimensional search space created by the interplay between sequencing and
                 speed selection. Although the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II
                 (NSGA-II) is effective in producing diverse Pareto-optimal solutions, it often
                 fails to scale efficiently for large or dynamic instances. Its uniform treatment
                 of the search space and lack of intelligent feedback mechanisms can lead to
                 excessive computation and suboptimal Pareto front coverage.      
                                                                                  
                 Recent advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly Explainable AI
                 (XAI) [2], offer a promising remedy. While research at the intersection of AI
                 and MOO is still emerging, learning-based methods can extract patterns from
                 explored solutions to predict promising search regions and adapt strategies
                 dynamically. Specifically, search space decomposition informed by
                 interpretable surrogate models may improve scalability by focusing
                 computational effort on high-potential areas while enhancing solution
                 diversity and algorithmic transparency.

                 Our proposed framework integrates periodic explainability mechanisms
                 within the NSGA-II process. Every T generations, surrogate models are
                 trained using eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) [3] to approximate
                 relationships between decision variables and the two objectives. SHapley
                 Additive exPlanations (SHAP) [2], specifically TreeSHAP [4], are used to
                 interpret feature influence—focusing on job sequencing and speed level
                 assignment. Based on SHAP value patterns, the population is clustered into
                 interpretable subspaces with similar decision–objective relationships.
                 Localized neighborhood searches are then applied within each cluster to
                 intensify exploration in high-potential regions.                 
                                                                                  
                 Preliminary experiments suggest that explainability improves optimization
                 efficiency and solution quality without significant computational time,
                 especially when SHAP analysis is scheduled periodically. The SHAP-driven
                 clustering helps NSGA-II focus on impactful decisions, reducing ineffective
                 evaluations. While runtime analysis is ongoing, early results indicate
                 promising scalability. We will present these preliminary findings at the
                 conference.
